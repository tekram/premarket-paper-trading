Methodology

The playbook

  • Each weekday premarket, Muse publishes a brief: market health, breadth, and 3–5 long / 2–3 short setups with catalysts.
  • Setups become paper trades filled at the regular-session open — never at a guessed price; unverifiable opens are skipped.
  • Risk rules are fixed: 8% stop, 15% target, 5-trading-day max hold. No discretion, no overrides.
  • First-touch accounting: if a session's range touches both stop and target, the exit is whichever printed first intraday (verified with intraday bars).

Dollar accounting

  • Every setup is booked at $10,000 notional. Dollar P&L per trade = $10,000 × per-trade %.
  • Totals are additive: total $ = $10,000 × (sum of per-trade %). The per-trade percentages are the signal; dollars make them tangible.
  • This is not a portfolio return. Summed percentages assume each trade stood alone — with up to 14 concurrent positions, gross notional has peaked at $140,000 against the $100,000 reference book. Read totals as signal P&L, not a literal account balance.

Is this day trading?

Not by design. Nothing is forced to close the same day — positions are short-term swings held 1 to 5 trading days, entered once at the open and managed only by the stop, target, or time stop. In practice these volatile names often resolve in a day or two when a level gets tagged, which can look like day trading on the tape, but there is no intraday entry/exit logic.

How Muse runs it

  • ~7:13 AM CT — premarket brief written (market health, breadth, setups).
  • 8:50 AM CT — morning logger verifies each setup's official open and books the paper trades.
  • 4:15 PM CT — exit check verifies every open position's session high/low/close, applies stops/targets/time exits, and publishes official marks.
  • This page rebuilds from the published trade files — nothing here is hand-edited.

Honest limits

Paper fills assume the open is attainable in size (it often isn't for thin names), there are no commissions or borrow costs on shorts, and gap-opens past a stop still exit at the stop level. Small sample: 5 closed trades so far — an 80% win rate means almost nothing yet. Treat this as a measure of the brief's edge, not a tradable return stream.
